> **Design guide:** This article explains construction and styling choices. It is not a complete stitch-by-stitch pattern; use a numbered pattern suited to your measurements when making the project.


Raffia-style yarn creates a crisp summer bag, but the fiber label matters. Paper raffia, viscose raffia and synthetic substitutes can respond differently to moisture and bending. Make a small sample and follow the manufacturer's care instructions before planning a bag that will carry daily essentials.

## Test the Fabric Under Load

Choose a dense stitch and hang a swatch with a modest weight attached. Watch for stretching at the top edge and gaps between stitches. If the fabric opens too much, adjust the hook or plan a lining. A decorative open fabric needs a separate pouch to keep small items contained.

## Build a Stable Base

Use a bag pattern with a base shape suited to the contents: an oval for a wider tote or a circle for a bucket bag. Check the base on a table as it grows. Keep increase markers visible; accidental extra stitches can make a permanent ruffle in this stiff material.

## Protect Handle Attachments

Mark handle positions before making the top border. Reinforce the attachment area across several stitches instead of relying on one loop. Purchased handles should have a smooth attachment surface that will not cut the yarn. If the handle is crocheted, test its stretch before final assembly.

## Finish the Interior

Fit a lining to the finished bag, allowing enough room for its shape without pulling the crochet inward. Hand-sew it just below the rim and reinforce pockets separately. Avoid steam or soaking unless the yarn label permits it. For a softer yarn alternative, see the [crochet tote construction guide](/patterns/crochet-tote-bag-pattern).
