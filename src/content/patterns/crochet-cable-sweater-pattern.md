Crochet cables use raised stitches to create crossing lines. Those crossings consume yarn and can draw the fabric inward, so a plain-stitch gauge sample cannot predict the size of a cable sweater. Use a graded cable pattern with a stitch chart and make your sample in its actual cable repeat.

## Read One Repeat Before Starting

Identify the background stitches, the post stitches and the crossing rows. Mark which stitches are skipped temporarily and which are worked later. Practice the crossing on a small sample so the order is clear before a large panel is on the hook.

## Keep the Crossing Loose Enough

Long post stitches need enough length to reach their destination without pulling the row downward. Keep the background tension steady and avoid tightening the final loop of a crossing. Inspect the sample from the front and back for missed stitches or an unintended twist.

## Measure After Washing

Block the sample gently, preserving the raised texture. Measure across complete repeats and compare with the pattern gauge. Adjust the hook and repeat the sample if necessary. Allow for the fabric's thickness when choosing ease and planning layers underneath.

## Align Panels and Sleeves

Track every row and mark the same reference point on matching panels. Before seaming, check that cable crossings line up at the intended height. Shape the neckline and armholes exactly as the graded pattern directs; changing a cable repeat can alter both width and shaping.

Keep seams flat and weave ends into the background fabric where possible. For a garment with simpler modular sections, see the [patchwork vest guide](/patterns/crochet-patchwork-sweater-vest).
