This classic faceless gnome is a holiday decorating staple — no embroidery skills needed since the long beard covers the face entirely. A cheerful, quick project for the season.

## Materials

- Red or green worsted weight yarn for the hat (about 25g)
- Cream or white yarn for the beard (about 25g)
- Skin-tone or peach yarn for the small visible nose (small amount)
- Neutral yarn (tan or grey) for the body, if not using fabric
- 4.0mm crochet hook
- Fiberfill stuffing
- Yarn needle

## Abbreviations

- **MR** — magic ring
- **sc** — single crochet
- **inc** — increase
- **dec** — invisible decrease
- **st(s)** — stitch(es)

## Body

Worked bottom-up in a continuous spiral, in your body/robe color.

1. MR, 6 sc in ring (6)
2. inc around (12)
3. (1 sc, inc) around (18)
4. (2 sc, inc) around (24)
5–14. sc around, no increase (24) — 10 rounds

Begin stuffing as you go, adding more toward the top.

15. (2 sc, dec) around (18)
16. (1 sc, dec) around (12)
17. dec around (6)

Fasten off, leaving a tail. Weave through remaining stitches and cinch closed — this becomes the top of the head, where the hat will attach.

## Nose

With skin-tone yarn:

1. MR, 6 sc in ring (6)
2. sc around (6)

Lightly stuff and fasten off leaving a tail. Sew to the center-front of the body, about a third of the way down from the top.

## Hat

Worked as a tall cone, in your chosen hat color.

1. MR, 6 sc in ring (6)
2. inc around (12)
3. sc around (12)
4. (1 sc, inc) around (18)
5. sc around (18)
6. (2 sc, inc) around (24)
7-12. sc around (24) — 6 rounds

Fasten off, leaving a long tail. The hat should be tall enough to droop slightly forward or to the side when placed on the body — this is the classic gnome silhouette.

## Beard

With cream or white yarn, cut strands approximately 10-15 cm long. Using a latch hook or simply folding each strand in half and pulling through with a crochet hook, attach strands densely across the lower two-thirds of the front of the body, just below and around the nose, working from the sides inward so the beard fully covers where a face would be.

Trim the beard to your desired length and shape — straight across for a tidy look, or a slight point for a more classic gnome beard.

## Assembly

Sew the hat to the top of the head, allowing it to flop slightly to one side or forward. Adjust the beard as needed to ensure full face coverage.

## Finishing

Weave in all ends. Optionally add a small pom-pom to the tip of the hat, or a thin belt around the middle of the body for extra detail.

*Tip: gnomes look best in odd-numbered groupings — make three in a slightly different hat color each for a natural-looking shelf display.*
