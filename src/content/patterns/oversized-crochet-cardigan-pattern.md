> **Design guide:** This article explains construction and styling choices. It is not a complete stitch-by-stitch pattern; use a numbered pattern suited to your measurements when making the project.


An oversized cardigan still needs intentional proportions. Decide whether the extra room should sit in the body, sleeves or dropped shoulders; increasing every dimension can produce cuffs that cover the hands and a neckline that slips off. Start with a graded cardigan pattern and use this guide to plan its fit.

## Measure a Layer You Already Like

Lay a comfortable cardigan flat and record body width, back length, armhole depth and sleeve length. Compare these with the chosen pattern's finished measurements. Account for the clothes worn underneath and the weight of the crochet fabric.

## Swatch for Drape

Make a generous stitch sample and wash it as the finished garment will be washed. Hang it briefly to see whether it lengthens under its own weight. A heavy yarn can turn an appealing small sample into a sagging full-length garment.

## Balance Body and Sleeves

With dropped shoulders, part of the sleeve length is already supplied by the wider body. Check the distance from the neck to the cuff before extending a sleeve. Baste panels together and try the garment on before committing to seams and bands.

## Stabilize the Opening

Work the front band with an even pickup rate and check it flat. Too many stitches cause waves; too few shorten the opening. Support button locations with a firm band and sew buttons through sufficient fabric. Recheck the hem after the garment rests overnight.
