A balaclava needs a clear face opening, comfortable neck coverage and enough room around the crown. Use a graded pattern intended for the wearer; a hat pattern with an improvised hole will not reliably place the eyes and mouth correctly.

## Map the Fit Points

Record head circumference, crown-to-chin length and the desired neck depth. Compare these with the pattern's finished dimensions. Decide whether the opening frames the full face or leaves only the eye area exposed, and choose a pattern designed for that arrangement.

## Test Warmth and Stretch

Wash a sample before measuring gauge. A dense fabric may be warm but feel restrictive around the jaw, while a looser fabric may lose its shape at the opening. Check the yarn against the skin at the cheek and neck before committing to the project.

## Check the Opening During Construction

At the stage specified by the pattern, try the piece on and confirm unobstructed vision and breathing. The fabric should not pull across the mouth when the wearer turns their head. Keep the opening symmetrical and record any permitted adjustments so both sides match.

## Finish the Face Edge Gently

Work the edging at a tension that holds the shape without drawing the opening inward. Avoid bulky knots at the temples or under the chin. Test the finished garment with the coat or hood it will accompany, then check that it can be removed easily.
