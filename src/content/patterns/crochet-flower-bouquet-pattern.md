A crochet bouquet combines separate flower heads with supported stems. Plan the arrangement before making dozens of petals: one large focal flower, a few smaller flowers and leaves can create a balanced gift. This construction guide helps assemble flowers made from individual flower patterns.

## Choose Three Flower Shapes

Roses need a curled petal strip or layered petals; tulips need a cup that holds its shape; daisies need a flat center with evenly spaced petals. Make one sample of each in the same yarn weight. Cotton usually gives clearer petal edges than fluffy yarn. Compare their finished sizes together before choosing quantities.

## Support the Flower Heads

Keep a rose's rolled base compact and stitch through all layers so the spiral cannot unwind. A tulip's opening should remain slightly cupped rather than stretched flat. For daisies, secure the petal ring to the center without pulling the petals into a bowl.

Attach each head to a covered floral wire or a wooden stem, enclosing every sharp end. Wrap or crochet a green sleeve around the support and secure both ends. These supported stems are display items rather than children's toys.

## Arrange Height and Color

Start with the focal flower. Add supporting flowers at different heights and rotate the bouquet while holding it loosely. Position leaves where they fill gaps rather than hiding the heads. Use a narrow palette if the flowers have very different textures.

## Wrap Without Crushing

Tie the stems firmly below the flowers, then add paper around the outside. Keep the upper wrap open so the petals remain visible. Test the arrangement in its intended vase before trimming stems. A [crochet basket](/patterns/scrap-yarn-basket-crochet-pattern) can hold a smaller arrangement when its base is weighted and stable.
