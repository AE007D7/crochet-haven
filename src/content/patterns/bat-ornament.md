This flat bat motif is a quick, satisfying make for Halloween decorating — string several into a garland, hang from a window, or use as a fun gift tag.

## Materials

- Black worsted weight yarn (about 10g per bat)
- Small amount of white or yellow yarn for eyes
- 4.0mm crochet hook
- Yarn needle
- Twine or ribbon for hanging

## Abbreviations

- **MR** — magic ring
- **sc** — single crochet
- **inc** — increase
- **ch** — chain
- **st(s)** — stitch(es)

## Body

1. MR, 6 sc in ring (6)
2. inc around (12)
3. sc around (12)

Fasten off, leaving a tail.

## Wings (make 2)

Chain 10.

**Row 1:** sc in 2nd ch from hook, hdc in next 2 sts, dc in next 3 sts, hdc in next 2 sts, sc in last st. This creates a curved, tapering wing shape.

For the classic scalloped bat-wing edge, work 2-3 small picots (ch 2, sl st back into the same stitch) along the outer curved edge before fastening off.

Fasten off, leaving a tail for sewing.

## Ears

Chain 3, sl st in 2nd ch from hook and next ch to form a tiny pointed triangle. Fasten off. Make a second to match.

## Assembly

Sew the wide end of each wing to either side of the round body. Sew the two small ear triangles to the top of the head/body.

## Eyes

With white or yellow yarn, embroider two small dot eyes on the front of the body, or use tiny seed beads sewn on securely.

## Finishing

Weave in all ends. Attach a small loop of twine or ribbon to the top for hanging.

*Tip: crochet a batch of 8-10 bats in one sitting and string them along a mantel — they're small enough to go quickly once you've made the first one.*
