A patchwork vest depends on a planned grid. Squares that look similar on the hook can change size after washing, so block samples before arranging the front and back. Use this guide alongside a sized vest pattern for neckline and armhole shaping.

## Establish One Square Size

Choose a motif and make several samples in the yarns you intend to use. Measure after washing and drying. If one color produces a noticeably larger square, adjust its hook or replace that yarn; forcing the mismatch into the seam can distort the vest.

## Draw the Patchwork Map

Lay out the back first, then divide the front around the neckline. Mark the shoulder joins and leave the armholes open. Place bold colors deliberately: a single bright square near the shoulder reads differently from a repeated checkerboard. Photograph the final arrangement and label the rows before stacking the pieces.

## Join in a Consistent Direction

Keep all motifs facing the same way unless rotation is part of the design. Join one row at a time with a seam that suits the intended drape. Check the whole panel flat after each row; a tight join can shorten the panel even when the individual squares are correct.

## Fit Before Adding Bands

Baste the shoulders and sides, then try the vest over the layer it will cover. Check the underarm opening and neckline depth. Follow your base pattern for partial motifs or shaped sections rather than filling every gap with a full square.

Pick up neck, armhole and hem bands evenly, adjusting the pickup count if they ripple or pinch. Compare a simpler single-motif layout in the [granny-square vest guide](/patterns/crochet-granny-square-vest-pattern).
